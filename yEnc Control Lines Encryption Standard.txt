SPECIFICATION: yEnc Control Lines Encryption Standard
Version: 1.2
Date: 2026-10-10
Author: Independent Specification

Status: Experimental Wire Contract (v1.2)
Category: Standards Track

Abstract:
This document defines a reversible, format-preserving encryption
scheme for yEnc control lines in yEnc-encoded binary blocks used in
Usenet transfers. The scheme maintains exact length for lines 2..N,
prepends an article bootstrap prefix on line 1 containing salt and
segmentIndex, and preserves the CR/LF-free 253-byte Alphabet range
required for Usenet transmission.

1. Scope

This specification describes the method to encrypt and decrypt
yEnc header and footer lines (lines beginning with "=y") while
maintaining:
  - yEnc-compatible characters (the 253-byte Alphabet: all bytes
    0x01-0xFF except 0x0A and 0x0D)
  - deterministic reconstruction of the original yEnc structure
  - exact length preservation for lines 2..N, with the first encrypted
    control line (lineIndex=1) expanding by exactly 20 bytes due to
    the prepended 20-byte article bootstrap (16-byte raw salt from
    the 253-byte Alphabet and 4-byte big-endian uint32 segmentIndex)
  - data lines remain completely untouched in length and byte content
  - format-preserving output suitable for Usenet transmission
  - no additional yEnc encoding step required after encryption
  - full compatibility and dual-bootstrap agreement with body encryption

It is intended for use in single or multipart binary uploads or
segment encryption, where yEnc control lines are encrypted while
preserving the overall yEnc structure and leaving data lines
unmodified. The overall yEnc block length increases by exactly 20 bytes
due to the bootstrap prefix prepended to the first line.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Definitions

  yEnc Block:
    A text segment beginning with "=ybegin" and ending with "=yend".

  yEnc Control Line:
    Any line within a yEnc block that begins with "=y" (such as
    "=ybegin", "=ypart", "=yend", or custom lines such as
    "=yencryption").

  Data Line:
    Any line within a yEnc block that does not begin with "=y"
    (contains the actual encoded binary data).

  Line Content:
    The text content of a line excluding any trailing LF (0x0A) or
    CRLF (0x0D 0x0A) line terminators.

  Alphabet:
    The 253-symbol byte domain: all byte values 0x01-0xFF except
    0x0A (LF) and 0x0D (CR).

    Canonical Numeral Bijection Table (Radix 253):
    To ensure cross-language interoperability, each byte b in Alphabet
    maps to an integer numeral I in [0, 252] strictly by ascending
    byte value:
      - For b in [0x01, 0x09] (9 values):   I = b - 1  (I in [0, 8])
      - For b = 0x0B (VT):                  I = 9
      - For b = 0x0C (FF):                  I = 10
      - For b in [0x0E, 0xFF] (242 values): I = b - 3  (I in [11, 252])

    Reverse Mapping (numeral I in [0, 252] to byte b in Alphabet):
      - For I <= 8:                         b = I + 1
      - For I = 9:                          b = 0x0B
      - For I = 10:                         b = 0x0C
      - For I >= 11:                        b = I + 3

  segmentIndex:
    An unsigned 32-bit integer in the range 1..=4294967295 encoded
    in the article bootstrap (bytes 16..19 of Line 1 prefix and the
    index parameter of =yencryption). It provides distinct tweak
    derivation inputs for every segment across the upload session without
    file-boundary arithmetic or NZB XML dependencies, yielding distinct
    derived tweaks with high probability across the session. With control
    line encryption enabled, a 4-byte big-endian uint32_be(segmentIndex)
    containing byte values 0x0A or 0x0D is forbidden because either byte
    would inject an NNTP line delimiter into the Line 1 prefix and split
    the bootstrap short. Uploaders MUST skip such indices, and
    decoders MUST reject the article under PROVIDER_FAILOVER.

   lineIndex:
     A 32-bit unsigned integer (1 <= lineIndex <= N) representing the
     1-based physical line number of every line (header, data, or
     footer) within the yEnc block after NNTP dot-unstuffing, where N
     is the 1-based line index of the last non-empty physical line of
     the dot-unstuffed article body; that line is the =yend footer
     line. Trailing empty lines after the footer are ignored before
     footer line counting: they are outside the yEnc block and are
     excluded from lineIndex accounting.
     Every line in the block, whether an encrypted control line or an
     unmodified data line, increments lineIndex sequentially by 1.
     The first line of the block has lineIndex = 1; the final footer
     line (=yend) has lineIndex = N.
    Used with segmentIndex to supply distinct derivation inputs for FF1
    tweaks. Because the 64-bit tweak is a truncated HMAC output, derived
    tweaks are distinct across articles and lines with high probability
    rather than by mathematical injectivity.

  yenc_encrypted Provenance:
    An explicit metadata flag in the NZB <head> section defined as
    <meta type="yenc_encrypted">true</meta>. It indicates that the
    underlying yEnc articles are wire-encrypted and instructs download
    clients to dispatch them to the transport decryption engine.

  Dual-Bootstrap Agreement:
    When control line encryption and body encryption are combined,
    the 16-byte raw salt and 4-byte uint32_be segmentIndex from Line 1
    MUST match the salt and index parameters encoded in the =yencryption
    header byte-for-byte and value-for-value. Because the two standards
    share one salt value, the combined-mode salt MUST lie in the
    intersection of the two salt domains, which is this standard's
    253-byte Alphabet: every shared salt byte MUST differ from 0x00,
    0x0A, and 0x0D. A producer that samples a forbidden byte for the
    shared salt cannot satisfy both standards and MUST resample.

  Four-GiB File Size Bound:
    An explicit bound requiring every encrypted file to be strictly
    smaller than 2^32 bytes (4 GiB); the producer and receiver
    obligations are the serialization rules of the body standard
    (Body Standard Section 2.1).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Processing Procedure

Input: Complete yEnc block with any line endings (LF or CRLF),
password, and segmentIndex assigned to this segment.

yEnc Structure Pattern:
- Header line(s): "=ybegin ...", "=ypart ..." (if multipart), "=yencryption ..."
- Data lines: encoded binary data (multiple lines)
- Footer line: "=yend ..."

The control-line and data-line roles of every physical line are a
function of position in this structure, not of line content. The
decryption procedure (Section 5) infers the layout from restored
line 2 and fixed positions; it never inspects data-line bytes
to locate boundaries.

Combined Mode Header Placement Rules:
In combined mode, =yencryption is ALWAYS present:
- An article is multipart if and only if physical line 2 after =ybegin is
  =ypart; otherwise it is single-part. Encrypted receivers NEVER read part=
  from =ybegin to decide header placement; they decrypt physical line 2 first.
- Single-part articles require =yencryption at physical line 2 immediately
  after =ybegin.
- Multipart articles require =ypart at physical line 2 and =yencryption
  at physical line 3 immediately after =ypart.
- Framing consistency: restored =ybegin MUST carry a part= keyword when
  physical line 2 is =ypart, and MUST NOT carry one when line 2 is
  =yencryption. A mismatch is corrupt framing and MUST fail under
  PROVIDER_FAILOVER. yEnc keywords on the restored =ybegin line are
  the space-separated tokens before name=; the name= value is the
  remainder of the line after name=, so text inside the name value
  (for example "part=") is never a keyword.
- The header MUST conform to the Canonical =yencryption Grammar in Section 4
  of the yEnc Body Encryption Standard. Its wire form is:
  =yencryption cipher=XChaCha20-Poly1305 salt=<32_hex_chars> index=<8_hex_chars> tag=<32_hex_chars>
  Each placeholder denotes lowercase hexadecimal characters and does not
  appear literally on the wire: salt is exactly 32 hex chars, index is
  exactly 8 hex chars (uint32_be in range 00000001..ffffffff), and tag is
  exactly 32 hex chars. Total line length is exactly 128 characters excluding CRLF.
  There is exactly one SP between the five tokens and no leading or trailing whitespace.
- Control line encryption processes the yEnc block after the =yencryption
  line has been inserted.

Encryption Input: Complete yEnc block with any line endings (LF or CRLF),
password, and the segmentIndex assigned to this segment.

Encryption follows the authoritative procedure in Section 4, applied to
each yEnc control line individually. Lines are split after NNTP
dot-unstuffing, preserving line ending information for reconstruction,
incrementing lineIndex on every physical line, whether control line or
data line. Data lines are left untouched, and all line
terminators and data lines are preserved exactly as received.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Encryption Algorithm

Algorithm: FF1 (NIST SP 800-38G compliant Format-Preserving Encryption)

Parameters:
  - Alphabet: 253 characters (all byte values 0x01-0xFF except
    0x0A and 0x0D; see Section 2)
  - Block cipher: AES-256
  - Salt: 16-byte random value sampled uniformly from the 253 Alphabet
    bytes (use cryptographically secure RNG). Every salt byte MUST be
    drawn from Alphabet. Reducing a full byte range modulo 253 without
    rejection produces a non-uniform salt and MUST NOT be used;
    rejection sampling, which re-draws any forbidden value, remains
    uniform and is permitted.
  - File Size: Encrypted files MUST be strictly smaller than 2^32 bytes
    (four-GiB bound). Producers MUST refuse larger files.
  - Key derivation: Argon2id(password, salt, time=1, memory=64MB,
    threads=4, 256-bit output). The exact KDF inputs are fixed and
    identical to the body standard's (Body Standard Section 4):
      * Password encoding: UTF-8, exactly as the source text, with
        NO Unicode normalization applied (no NFC, NFD, NFKC, NFKD).
      * Argon2id version: 0x13 (decimal 19, RFC 9106 v=19).
      * time cost (iterations/t): 1.
      * memory cost (m): 65536 KiB (64 MiB).
      * parallelism (p/lanes): 4.
      * Tag length: 32 bytes (256 bits).
      * Optional secret (K) input: empty (zero-length).
      * Optional associated data (X) input: empty (zero-length).
    Any other KDF parameter set MUST be rejected as a locally
    unsupported cryptographic configuration (structural tier).
  - XML representability of passwords: as in the body standard
    (Body Standard Section 4). The transport password carried by
    <meta type="password"> MUST parse back to exactly the sequence
    of Unicode code points that is UTF-8 encoded into the KDF.
    Parsers MUST NOT silently alter the KDF password (no stripping,
    trimming, case folding, normalization, or character dropping).
    Producers MUST refuse XML-unrepresentable passwords on the NZB
    route before posting; consumers MUST treat a password that fails
    XML parsing as METADATA_VALIDATION.
  - Tweak: HMAC-SHA256(masterKey, "yenc-control tweak" ||
    uint32_be(segmentIndex) || uint32_be(lineIndex))[0:8]
    where segmentIndex is the unsigned 32-bit integer in range 1..=4294967295
    assigned to the segment. The 4-byte uint32_be(segmentIndex)
    MUST NOT contain byte values 0x0A or 0x0D;
    uploaders MUST skip any index containing 0x0A or 0x0D in its 4 big-endian bytes,
    and decoders MUST reject such an index under PROVIDER_FAILOVER
    (for example indices 10, 13, 266, and 269).

Procedure (applied to each yEnc control line individually):

   a. Generate salt = Random 16-byte value sampled uniformly from the
      253 Alphabet bytes (once per upload session, use cryptographically
      secure RNG; use rejection sampling, drawing again on any byte
      outside Alphabet).
  b. Derive masterKey = Argon2id(password encoded to UTF-8 with no
     normalization, salt, time=1, memory=65536 KiB, parallelism=4,
     32-byte output, version 0x13, empty optional secret and
     associated data; exact inputs per this Section).
  c. Derive encKey = HMAC-SHA256(masterKey, "yenc-control key").
     The message input to HMAC-SHA256 is exactly 16 bytes: the ASCII
     prefix "yenc-control key". The encKey is the full 32-byte digest,
     used as the AES-256 key for FF1.
  d. Derive tweak = HMAC-SHA256(masterKey, "yenc-control tweak" ||
     uint32_be(segmentIndex) || uint32_be(lineIndex))[0:8].
     The message input to HMAC-SHA256 is exactly 26 bytes:
       - Bytes 0..17 (18 bytes): ASCII prefix "yenc-control tweak"
       - Bytes 18..21 (4 bytes): uint32_be(segmentIndex) (4-byte
         unsigned integer in big-endian network byte order)
       - Bytes 22..25 (4 bytes): uint32_be(lineIndex) (4-byte
         unsigned integer in big-endian network byte order)
     The tweak is the first 8 bytes of the resulting HMAC-SHA256
     digest, passed as the FF1 tweak input.
  e. Encrypt the line content using FF1 over the 253-character Alphabet.
  f. For the first line (lineIndex=1): prepend the 20-byte article bootstrap
     (16-byte raw salt from Alphabet || 4-byte uint32_be(segmentIndex)) to
     the encrypted content.
  g. Replace the original line content with the encrypted content
     (including 20-byte bootstrap prefix for first line).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Decryption and Error Handling

   Input: yEnc block with encrypted control lines and the transport
   password. N is the 1-based line index of the last non-empty physical
   line of the dot-unstuffed article body; that line is the =yend
   footer line. Trailing empty lines after the footer are ignored
   before footer line counting: they are outside the yEnc block and are
   excluded from lineIndex accounting.

Decryption Steps:

1. Inspect NZB metadata: verify <meta type="yenc_encrypted">true</meta> is
   present in the NZB <head> (trimmed, ASCII case-insensitive; any other
   value means ordinary yEnc — Body Standard Section 8).
2. Split the dot-unstuffed yEnc block into individual lines, preserving
   line endings (accepting CRLF and bare LF).
3. Process line 1 (lineIndex=1) to extract article bootstrap:
   a. Extract line 1 content (excluding line terminators). This content is
      taken after NNTP dot-unstuffing; the 20-byte bootstrap layout and the
      >= 22-byte length check both apply to the unstuffed content, so a
      dot-stuffed leading byte (0x2E doubled) is removed before extraction.
   b. Verify line 1 contains at least 22 bytes (20-byte bootstrap + minimum
       2-byte FF1 ciphertext). If shorter, reject as LINE_TRUNCATED.
    c. Extract the 16-byte raw salt and the 4-byte big-endian segmentIndex
       from the 20-byte bootstrap prefix (the layout defined in Sections 2
       and 4), asserting no forbidden bytes (0x00, 0x0A, 0x0D) in the 16-byte salt
       and a valid segmentIndex in range 1..=4294967295. Assert that the 4-byte
       uint32_be(segmentIndex) contains no forbidden delimiter bytes (0x0A or 0x0D)
       and reject a forbidden value under PROVIDER_FAILOVER (reject zero as
       ZERO_SEGMENT_INDEX; reject a forbidden salt byte as
       INVALID_SALT_CHARACTER).
   d. Remove the 20-byte bootstrap prefix (bytes 20.. are the FF1 ciphertext).
   e. Derive masterKey, encKey, and tweak using extracted salt, extracted
      segmentIndex, and lineIndex=1.
   f. Apply FF1 decryption over the 253-character Alphabet.
   g. Verify decrypted content starts with "=ybegin". If it does not,
      classify error as PROVIDER_FAILOVER (token CONTROL_LINE_DECRYPT_FAILURE)
      and release zero bytes.
   h. Replace line 1 encrypted content with decrypted content.
4. Determine the header layout deterministically: in combined mode (the
   normative v1.2 contract), physical line 2 is always an encrypted
   control line. The receiver decrypts physical line 2 first (lineIndex=2)
   using encKey and the tweak derived for lineIndex=2, before deciding placement.
   a. If line 2 decrypts to "=ypart ...":
      The article is multipart: line 2 is =ypart. The receiver then
      decrypts physical line 3 (lineIndex=3) with the tweak derived for
      lineIndex=3, which MUST decrypt to "=yencryption ...". First data
      line index D is 4.
      Framing consistency check: verify that the restored =ybegin line
      carries a part= parameter. If restored =ybegin lacks a part=
      parameter, framing is contradictory; reject the article under
      PROVIDER_FAILOVER (token INVALID_MULTIPART_FRAMING).
   b. If line 2 decrypts to "=yencryption ...":
      The article is single-part: line 2 is =yencryption. First data line
      index D is 3.
      Framing consistency check: verify that the restored =ybegin line
      does NOT carry a part= parameter. If restored =ybegin carries a
      part= parameter, framing is contradictory; reject the article under
      PROVIDER_FAILOVER (token MISPLACED_ENCRYPTION_HEADER).
   c. If line 2 decrypts to neither "=ypart" nor "=yencryption":
      Decryption has failed (e.g. wrong password or corrupt line); reject
      under PROVIDER_FAILOVER (token CONTROL_LINE_DECRYPT_FAILURE).
   d. For the restored =yencryption line, validate the canonical
      five-token grammar from the body standard and assert
      dual-bootstrap agreement: decoded header salt MUST match Line 1
      raw salt byte-for-byte AND decoded header index MUST match
      Line 1 segmentIndex value-for-value. An FF1 decryption error, a
      grammar violation, a placement disagreement, or a dual-bootstrap
      mismatch is PROVIDER_FAILOVER.
   e. Bounded file size check: if any of the fields enumerated in
      Body Standard Section 2.1 (the =ypart begin parameter, the
      =ypart end parameter, or the =ybegin size= parameter) is
      >= 2^32, reject under PROVIDER_FAILOVER (token SIZE_OVERFLOW).
      If begin < 1 or end < begin, reject under PROVIDER_FAILOVER.
   f. Every physical line between the last expected header position
      and the footer position N (that is, lineIndex D through N - 1)
      is a data line: it MUST be passed through completely untouched,
      byte for byte, including its line terminator. Data lines are
      NEVER decrypted, never trial-decrypted, and never inspected to
      locate the header/footer boundary. A data line whose bytes
      happen to begin with "=y" remains a data line; genuine control
      lines are recognizable by position alone.
   g. The footer is identified deterministically by position:
      the last non-empty physical line of the dot-unstuffed article
      body (lineIndex = N) is the =yend footer. No content-based
      footer search is performed.
   h. Out of scope for v1.2: control-only articles (control line
      encryption without body encryption), articles with custom
      control lines other than =ybegin, =ypart, =yencryption, and
      =yend, and header layouts not fixed by restored line 2. A
      producer MUST NOT emit such articles in v1.2. Outside a
      claim of v1.2 conformance, a receiver MAY implement additional
      layouts and decrypt the positions its own layout predicts;
      this never overrides Body Standard Section 5. A v1.2 receiver
      that receives such an article classifies it as
      PROVIDER_FAILOVER.
5. Pass through data lines (lineIndex = D through N - 1):
   - Leave all data lines untouched, incrementing lineIndex for each 1-based
     physical line.
6. Process the final line (footer, lineIndex = N):
   a. Extract the content of line N (excluding line terminators).
   b. Derive tweak using extracted salt, extracted segmentIndex, and lineIndex = N.
   c. Apply FF1 decryption over the 253-character Alphabet.
   d. Verify decrypted content starts with "=yend". Any other result,
      including an FF1 decryption error, is PROVIDER_FAILOVER.
   e. Replace line N encrypted content with decrypted content.
7. Reconstruct the complete yEnc block with original line endings.
8. Proceed to body decryption in the order required by the body standard
   (Body Standard Section 5): decode yEnc data lines to ciphertext, verify
   the applicable wire CRC over ciphertext (pcrc32 for multipart, crc32 for
   single-part) before authentication, then perform Poly1305 authentication
   and decryption, and finally apply downstream CRC normalization: decoders
   MUST either clear the CRC metadata
   (crc32 = None) or recompute it over authenticated plaintext.

Two-Tier Error Handling:

The provider-corruption tier is identified as PROVIDER_FAILOVER and the
structural tier as METADATA_VALIDATION in error surfaces; these identifiers
name the two tiers defined below and are used consistently with the
README and with the body standard.

An implementation MUST classify a failure by the input that failed
validation, exactly once, identically to the body standard (Body
Standard Section 5). The structural tier (METADATA_VALIDATION) applies
to NZB metadata and local configuration validated before an article
fetch, including locally unsupported KDF parameters. The provider-corruption tier
(PROVIDER_FAILOVER) applies to bytes in a fetched article after those
structural inputs have passed validation. A restored control line
that fails syntax, placement, or cross-header checks is therefore
provider corruption, as is a fetched article whose restored structure
the receiver cannot parse. This rule prevents the same defect from
being classified in both tiers.

Effective password source: as in the body standard (Body Standard
Section 5). <meta type="password"> in the NZB is the interoperable
default; clients MAY override it from their own sources resolved
before fetch; a missing password after resolution is structural;
the effective password feeds the KDF unaltered.

1. Non-Retriable Structural Failures (structural tier / METADATA_VALIDATION):
   - Missing NZB provenance when transport decryption was requested (MISSING_ENCRYPTION_PROVENANCE).
   - Malformed NZB XML or invalid provenance metadata (INVALID_ENCRYPTION_PROVENANCE).
   - No transport password available in NZB or client configuration (MISSING_PASSWORD).
   - Unsupported specification version (UNSUPPORTED_VERSION).
   - An unsupported cipher suite in NZB metadata or local configuration, validated before an article fetch (UNSUPPORTED_CIPHER). An unsupported cipher token inside a fetched =yencryption line is provider corruption, not a structural failure (see the retriable tier below).
   - Unsupported Argon2id KDF parameters (UNSUPPORTED_KDF).
   Action: Abort the job without querying alternate Usenet servers.
   Output: Zero bytes of plaintext and zero bytes of ciphertext.

2. Retriable Provider Corruption Failures:
   - Restored Line 1 does not begin with =ybegin (CONTROL_LINE_DECRYPT_FAILURE).
   - Restored Line N does not begin with =yend (CONTROL_LINE_DECRYPT_FAILURE).
   - Line 1 shorter than 22 bytes (LINE_TRUNCATED).
   - Control line shorter than 2-byte FF1 minimum (LINE_TOO_SHORT).
   - Zero segmentIndex in Line 1 bootstrap (ZERO_SEGMENT_INDEX).
   - Forbidden byte (0x00, 0x0A, 0x0D) in Line 1 salt (INVALID_SALT_CHARACTER).
   - Delimiter byte (0x0A, 0x0D) in 4-byte uint32_be(segmentIndex) field (FORBIDDEN_SEGMENT_INDEX_BYTE).
   - Missing, malformed, misplaced, or duplicate yEnc control lines in the fetched article at the deterministic positions, including =ypart and =yencryption.
   - An =yencryption line that violates the canonical five-token grammar
     or contains an unsupported cipher token other than XChaCha20-Poly1305
     (UNSUPPORTED_CIPHER).
   - Line 2 decrypts to neither =ypart nor =yencryption (CONTROL_LINE_DECRYPT_FAILURE).
   - Line 2 is =ypart but =ybegin has no part= parameter (INVALID_MULTIPART_FRAMING).
   - Line 2 is =yencryption but =ybegin carries part= parameter (MISPLACED_ENCRYPTION_HEADER).
   - Whitespace defects (tabs, consecutive spaces) in =yencryption (INVALID_WHITESPACE).
   - Invalid token count or empty tokens in =yencryption (INVALID_TOKEN_COUNT).
   - Invalid header prefix (INVALID_PREFIX).
   - Invalid token ordering in =yencryption (INVALID_TOKEN_ORDER).
   - Invalid salt length or hex in =yencryption (INVALID_SALT_LENGTH, INVALID_SALT_HEX).
   - Invalid index length or hex in =yencryption (INVALID_INDEX_LENGTH, INVALID_INDEX_HEX).
   - Invalid tag length or hex in =yencryption (INVALID_TAG_LENGTH, INVALID_TAG_HEX).
   - Uppercase hex in =yencryption tokens (UPPERCASE_HEX).
   - Dual-salt mismatch between Line 1 and =yencryption (DUAL_SALT_MISMATCH).
   - Dual-index mismatch between Line 1 and =yencryption (DUAL_INDEX_MISMATCH).
   - File size or segment offset >= 2^32 bytes (SIZE_OVERFLOW).
   - Wire CRC mismatch over decoded ciphertext (WIRE_CRC_MISMATCH).
   - Poly1305 authentication tag verification failure (AUTHENTICATION_FAILURE).
   - Article body truncation or missing bytes.
   Action: Mark the article corrupt and try an eligible alternate provider.
   After all eligible providers fail, mark the segment failed so normal repair
   policy can run.
   Output: Zero bytes of plaintext and zero bytes of ciphertext.

A decoder MUST NOT release or write unauthenticated plaintext or ciphertext
to a target file, cache visible to downstream stages, or subsequent pipeline
stage. Release means any transfer of unauthenticated payload bytes or
unvalidated article data outside the validation pipeline to downstream
components, streams, pipes, buffers shared with subsequent pipeline
stages, or observable sinks, not only the final file write. Internal
restored control metadata, intermediate decoded ciphertext, and
article-sized working buffers MAY pass between internal helpers inside
the article validation and decryption pipeline (for example, to reconstruct
control lines, infer framing, derive Associated Data, verify wire CRC, and
perform body authentication). What is strictly forbidden is releasing
unauthenticated payload bytes (plaintext or ciphertext) or unvalidated
article structure to assembly, downstream caches, files, or observable
output before validation completes. Partial release of unauthenticated
payload during decryption, before validation completes, is a violation of
this rule. An implementation MAY retain article-sized working buffers
until validation completes and MUST discard them on failure.

6. Length Properties

- The first encrypted yEnc control line (lineIndex=1) expands by
  exactly 20 bytes due to the prepended article bootstrap (16 bytes
  salt + 4 bytes big-endian segmentIndex). This expansion is measured on
  the line content, defined in Section 2 as the line excluding its
  trailing line terminator; the terminator is neither counted in the
  20-byte expansion nor altered.
- All subsequent encrypted yEnc control lines (lineIndex > 1) preserve
  exact plaintext length (identical length to original).
- Data lines remain completely unchanged in length, byte content,
  and line terminators.
- Overall yEnc block length increases by exactly 20 bytes (the bootstrap).
- Line endings (CRLF or LF) and data line formatting are preserved
  throughout the process: restoration preserves, byte for byte, the
  terminators the restoration layer receives, so a bare-LF block
  delivered to restoration is restored with bare LF and a CRLF block
  with CRLF. The restoration layer itself MUST NOT canonicalize bare
  LF to CRLF. A transport layer MAY have normalized bare LF to CRLF
  before restoration (Body Standard Section 9); restoration is not
  judged against terminators that transport already replaced. In all
  cases line contents, the physical line count, the bootstrap bytes,
  the wire CRC, the AAD, and the authenticated plaintext MUST remain
  unchanged. Restoration APIs that receive raw terminators MUST
  preserve them exactly.

7. Security Considerations

- FF1 provides format-preserving confidentiality constrained by its
  domain (the 253-character Alphabet); its security builds on the
  underlying AES-256 block cipher under key/tweak pairs that are
  practically distinct.
- Distinct segmentIndex and lineIndex values ensure distinct HMAC-SHA256
  derivation inputs. Because the 64-bit tweak is a truncated HMAC-SHA256
  output, distinct tweaks across all articles and lines are probabilistic
  (pseudorandom) rather than mathematically guaranteed: collision
  probability follows the birthday bound over the number of control lines
  under one key, and is negligible at the article counts and line counts
  typical of a single upload session. This is not an anti-replay design;
  receiver queue and coverage checks remain responsible for freshness
  (Body Standard Sections 2.1 and 7).
- Argon2id with random salts provides strong protection against brute-force
  and rainbow table attacks.
- Dual-bootstrap verification prevents salt or index substitution attacks
  across control line and body encryption schemes.
- The scheme offers confidentiality for control headers and footers.
- segmentIndex is public metadata self-described within article bootstrap
  bytes; it does not require confidentiality.
- Tampering with segmentIndex produces an incorrect tweak, causing
  FF1 to output arbitrary bytes that do not begin with "=ybegin"
  on line 1, failing restoration.
- Restoration is deterministic and position-based: data lines are
  never decrypted, so a modified data line cannot probe the FF1
  keystream or leak decryption success/failure oracles. The FF1
  layer carries no content authentication; in combined mode the
  body standard's associated data (Body Standard Section 2.1)
  provides position binding, and the restored control lines have no
  cryptographic integrity guarantee of their own.
- Subject lines, XML element order, and NNTP header order have no
  cryptographic role, enabling full subject and metadata obfuscation.
  (The =ybegin name value is authenticated by the body AEAD; see the
  Body Standard Sections 2.1 and 7.)
- The body AEAD authenticates an article against its own restored
  control lines only: an intact article carries its own valid AAD and
  has no cryptographic freshness binding to a Message-ID, an NZB
  slot, or any external record. Receiver queue and coverage checks
  remain responsible for segment coverage and ordering (Body Standard
  Section 2.1).
- The KDF password is used exactly as delivered (UTF-8, no
  normalization); implementations that alter it before derivation
  break interoperability.
- All failure paths enforce zero output, preventing chosen-ciphertext
  or partial-plaintext leakage.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
8. NZB File Requirements

NZB metadata, provenance and password carriers, standard NZB 1.1
segments, producer requirements, consumer requirements, and
forward-compatibility metadata gating are those of the body standard
(Body Standard Section 8). The Control standard adds one consumer
obligation:

- Use the extracted segmentIndex for control-line tweak derivation as
  well as body nonce derivation.

Decoupling of Presentation Metadata:
- <file subject> has no cryptographic meaning.
- NNTP Subject headers have no cryptographic meaning.
- XML file order and segment order have no cryptographic meaning.
- Subject obfuscation does not impair decryption.

9. Interoperability Notes

- Any FF1 implementation conforming to NIST SP 800-38G may be used
  if it supports custom alphabets.
- Implementations MUST use the canonical 253-byte Alphabet numeral
  bijection specified in Section 2, including its forward mapping
  (byte octets b to numerals I in [0, 252]) and reverse mapping
  (numerals I to byte octets b).
- Physical Line Indexing: Line indices are counted on 1-based physical
  lines after NNTP dot-unstuffing has been applied.
- Line Terminators: Canonical yEnc generators emit CRLF (0x0D 0x0A).
  Decoders MUST accept both CRLF and bare LF (0x0A) transparently.
- Transport boundary: Producers MUST dot-stuff article content per
  RFC 3977 Section 3.1.1; consumers MUST dot-unstuff before line splitting
  and index counting. The <segment bytes> and size= parameters report
  dot-unstuffed content length, not wire length.
- Article body boundary: Line 1 (lineIndex=1) is the first line of the
  article body: the first response line for BODY (222), or the line
  after the first empty line ending the headers for ARTICLE (220),
  as in Body Standard Section 9. A receiver MAY fetch with BODY only
  and reject an ARTICLE response under PROVIDER_FAILOVER. Decoders MUST
  NOT locate it by trial decryption of header lines.
- Article-Bounded Memory: An implementation MAY buffer one complete article
  while restoring control lines and authenticating an encrypted body. It MUST
  keep working memory proportional to the configured maximum article size,
  O(article_size), and MUST NOT accumulate a complete multipart file or release
  in memory. Reference implementations accept typical article sizes from
  128 KiB through 4 MiB and enforce local safety limits up to 8 MiB. These
  sizes are implementation guidance, not wire-format limits. A receiver MAY
  reject an article above its configured limit before allocation or decryption.

10. Example

This example demonstrates combined mode (control line encryption and
body encryption) using the single-part article from Section 10 of the
yEnc Body Encryption Standard.

Input binary data: [16 bytes: 0x48656C6C6F20576F726C642E747874FF]
Password: "public-conformance-vector-key-2026"

Corresponding NZB 1.1 XML:

<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<!DOCTYPE nzb PUBLIC "-//newzBin//DTD NZB 1.1//EN" "http://www.newzbin.com/DTD/nzb/nzb-1.1.dtd">
<nzb xmlns="http://www.newzbin.com/DTD/2003/nzb">
  <head>
    <meta type="yenc_encrypted">true</meta>
    <meta type="password">public-conformance-vector-key-2026</meta>
  </head>
  <file poster="uploader@example.com" date="1758998400" subject="test upload [1/1] - file.bin yEnc (1/1)">
    <groups>
      <group>alt.binaries.test</group>
    </groups>
    <segments>
      <segment bytes="16" number="1">article-1@example.com</segment>
    </segments>
  </file>
</nzb>

Cryptographic Derivations:
- password: "public-conformance-vector-key-2026" (UTF-8, no normalization)
- salt: 1a2b3c4d5e6f7890abcdef1234567890 (16 raw Alphabet bytes)
- segmentIndex: 1 (uint32_be: 0x00000001)
- masterKey = Argon2id("public-conformance-vector-key-2026", salt, time=1, memory=65536 KiB,
  parallelism=4, 32-byte output, version 0x13):
  bb3c0122434bc79ebcda18353ea17c68b8b0f4c9f4fe1758d9d94cdd1fbc2a85
- encKey = HMAC-SHA256(masterKey, "yenc-control key"):
  97e1d042fb40b75d43def00368220e5d31ddfe2560152dd25d97c4cc05af4844

Wire Block Structure (Single-Part Combined Mode, 4 physical lines):

Line 1 (lineIndex=1):
  Plaintext (38 bytes):
    =ybegin line=128 size=16 name=file.bin
  tweak = HMAC-SHA256(masterKey, "yenc-control tweak" || uint32_be(1) || uint32_be(1))[0:8]:
    129591ab1b3982e3
  FF1 Ciphertext (38 bytes hex):
    4c41e52d869349d1f8021adcc9ed165969f097198ebe3cb941e2f822d93ed8af4f42cd0440cf
  Wire Line 1 (58 bytes = 20-byte bootstrap prefix + 38 bytes ciphertext, hex):
    1a2b3c4d5e6f7890abcdef1234567890000000014c41e52d869349d1f8021adcc9ed165969f097198ebe3cb941e2f822d93ed8af4f42cd0440cf

Line 2 (lineIndex=2):
  Plaintext (128 bytes):
    =yencryption cipher=XChaCha20-Poly1305 salt=1a2b3c4d5e6f7890abcdef1234567890 index=00000001 tag=8a6d1659c13f895a17cbe0d2a463133e
  tweak = HMAC-SHA256(masterKey, "yenc-control tweak" || uint32_be(1) || uint32_be(2))[0:8]:
    38490a579405b8d5
  Wire Line 2 / FF1 Ciphertext (128 bytes hex):
    202ab1f6ce0113e76ed64ff791b4eb8e6297a4af5b619955ddb8c6d977caca7162cb8badb76eebcd022c6505911805e19b69670e98f9c24fe92d06a4d759b428dcf26726bb5a8c5656d6f2be241fa9383a093e1133f0525431d96f41960c685fb0c444ba8a6b7a54096b49c20f1d427e5442d6ace12dfa5b27a98be641a53ab1

Line 3 (lineIndex=3):
  Untouched data line (yEnc-encoded ciphertext bytes): passes through
  byte-for-byte; never decrypted by control line processing.

Line 4 (lineIndex=4, footer):
  Plaintext (28 bytes):
    =yend size=16 crc32=03c5ab1e
  tweak = HMAC-SHA256(masterKey, "yenc-control tweak" || uint32_be(1) || uint32_be(4))[0:8]:
    256f329858111c27
  Wire Line 4 / FF1 Ciphertext (28 bytes hex):
    5ffcabd3cadf34d47d1db8c88395e6fa91c11fa61c64504a9d3260d4

Decryption Processing:
- Line 1 (lineIndex=1):
  * Extract 16-byte raw salt and 4-byte uint32_be segmentIndex=1.
  * Decrypt ciphertext (bytes 20..) with lineIndex=1 tweak.
  * Restores: "=ybegin line=128 size=16 name=file.bin".
- Line 2 (lineIndex=2):
  * Receiver decrypts line 2 first with lineIndex=2 tweak.
  * Restores: "=yencryption cipher=XChaCha20-Poly1305 salt=1a2b... index=00000001 tag=daad...".
  * Because line 2 decrypted to =yencryption (not =ypart), this article is single-part.
  * Verify that restored =ybegin has no part= parameter (consistent framing).
  * Assert dual-bootstrap agreement between Line 1 and =yencryption.
- Line 3 (lineIndex=3):
  * First data line (D = 3); passes through untouched to body decryption.
- Line 4 (lineIndex=4 = N):
  * Identified as footer by position (last non-empty physical line).
  * Decrypt with lineIndex=4 tweak.
  * Restores: "=yend size=16 crc32=03c5ab1e".

After control line restoration, the receiver proceeds to body decryption,
wire CRC check, Poly1305 authentication under Section 2.1 AAD, and downstream
CRC normalization.

11. Summary

This Standard describes deterministic format-preserving encryption
(FF1) applied selectively to yEnc control lines, producing
encrypted messages that:
  - Keep all data lines completely unchanged.
  - Encrypt only yEnc header and footer information (lines starting with "=y").
  - Restore control lines deterministically by position, never by
    trial-decrypting data lines (Section 5).
  - Prepend 20-byte article bootstrap prefix on Line 1 (16B salt + 4B segmentIndex)
    while preserving exact length on lines 2..N.
  - Maintain dual-bootstrap agreement when combined with body encryption.
  - Follow strict header placement rules for single-part (line 2) and multipart (line 3).
  - Require <meta type="yenc_encrypted">true</meta> in NZB metadata.
  - Carry segment identity in the article bootstrap, leaving NZB segments standard.
  - Preserve exact message structure and line endings (CRLF canonical, bare LF accepted).
  - Remain within the CR/LF-free 253-byte Alphabet and safe for Usenet transport.
  - Enforce two-tier error handling with zero output on failure.
